(03) — System types

Three kinds of solar. One is right for you.

Don't worry about the technical names — the tabs below explain each type in plain language. 9 out of 10 homes we install are simple on-grid systems.

On-Grid — the most popular choice

Your solar is connected to the electricity department's (DISCOM's) grid. Extra daytime power flows to the grid and your meter runs backwards — net metering. At night you use grid power as usual.

  • Cheapest option — no battery cost, fastest payback (3–5 years)
  • Eligible for PM Surya Ghar subsidy up to ₹78,000
  • Best when your area has few power cuts
  • Doesn't work during power cuts (safety shut-off) — choose Hybrid for that

Off-Grid — full independence

No grid connection at all. Panels charge a battery bank through a solar PCU, and your home or farm runs from the battery. Perfect for farms, borewells, fencing and places the electricity line hasn't reached.

  • Fully self-reliant — works anywhere the sun shines
  • Great for agriculture motors, gates, fencing, farm huts
  • Battery adds cost and needs replacement every 5–8 years
  • Not eligible for the grid-tied PM Surya Ghar subsidy

Hybrid — grid + battery, best of both

A hybrid inverter does both jobs: exports extra solar to the grid for bill savings and keeps a battery charged for power cuts. Costs more, but nothing in your house switches off — ever.

  • Bill savings + backup during power cuts
  • Smart presets: battery first, grid first, or export first
  • Ideal for homes with lifts, medical equipment, WFH setups
  • Highest initial cost — battery sizing should be done carefully

(08) — Common questions

Asked by every family. Answered simply.

If your question isn't here, just call 99627 94336 — we'd rather talk for ten minutes than have you guess.

Your new meter counts in both directions. When your panels make extra power (usually afternoon), it flows to the grid and the meter counts backwards. At night you use grid power and it counts forward. At month-end you pay only for the difference — which is usually almost zero.

And it gets better: the extra units you export are kept as credit in your account (called "banking"), so sunny months help pay for cloudy months. In Telangana and AP, these banked units are typically carried forward for up to a financial year as per DISCOM rules.

Under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana for residential on-grid systems:

The subsidy is credited directly to your bank account after inspection and commissioning — usually within about 30 days. We register your application on the national portal, upload documents, track approvals and follow up till the money lands. You never pay us anything for this service.

A normal on-grid system switches off during cuts — this is a safety rule that protects electricity line-men working on the pole near you. The panels are fine; the system just pauses.

If cuts are common in your area, we recommend a hybrid system: it keeps a battery charged and powers your fans, lights, TV, router and even fridge while the grid is down — with automatic changeover so fast the lights barely flicker. In our experience, most families only need 1.5–3 kWh of battery for comfortable evening backup.
